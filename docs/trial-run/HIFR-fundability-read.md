# Fundability Read — Home Is the Foundation Restoration

*Prepared 2026-08-20 · Free assessment, no obligation · Sources: the organization's business plan (provided 2026-08-20) and the verified opportunity research in the companion sheet. Nothing here predicts an award or promises an outcome.*

## The short version

This organization is more fundable than most startups we read — but not yet through the front door most people picture. The mission (critical dry-in home repair for elderly and low-income homeowners in an ARC-distressed county) sits squarely inside what a real set of funders pays for. The constraint is structural, not substantive: operating as a 508(c)(1)(A) without an IRS determination letter closes roughly a third of the researched market — specifically the private-foundation and corporate cash-grant third. The strategy that fits the next 12 months is therefore partnerships and homeowner-direct dollars first, small direct grants second, and the foundation tier through a fiscal sponsor until the planned 501(c)(3) CDC exists.

## What works in your favor

1. **Geography multiplies eligibility.** Morgan County is ARC-distressed and USDA-rural. Several programs in the research cite exactly these designations, and the distressed designation raises the federal cost-share on ARC projects.
2. **The niche is a real gap, not a crowded lane.** Weatherization crews must *defer* homes with roof leaks or structural failure — your exact scope. That makes you the missing piece for an existing federal program partner headquartered in your own town, rather than a competitor to anyone.
3. **The model matches funder language.** Sweat equity, mutual aid, beneficiary participation, and trades mentorship map directly onto specific program criteria (one national denominational grant is gated on beneficiary control, not tax status).
4. **The materials pipeline is an asset.** Reclaimed-materials supply and volunteer labor mean small dollar amounts convert into whole finished repairs — a $250 fraternal micro-grant genuinely equals one ramp. Funders notice that arithmetic.

## What works against you — said plainly

1. **No determination letter.** Most private foundations, several corporate portals with automated IRS checks, and the strongest national affiliations require one. This is the single biggest gate, and no drafting skill removes it.
2. **No track record, staff, or financial systems yet.** First applications need extra lead time for organizational documents, and some funders will read "startup" as risk regardless of merit.
3. **No SAM.gov/UEI registration.** Federal org-facing programs need it and registration can take weeks — it should start now even though no federal application is imminent.
4. **Documentation of need is anecdotal so far.** A photographed, consented case file per homeowner (condition, scope, income band, age) is the raw material nearly every application in the plan will ask for. Start the case log immediately — one program's three-year service-history clock only starts when the log does.

## What has to be true before an application makes sense

- The **data room** items in the companion checklist exist (EIN paperwork, organizing documents, board list, a simple budget, the case log).
- For the foundation tier: a **fiscal sponsor** is signed (Frontier Housing is the natural candidate) **or** the CDC's 1023-EZ is filed.
- For federal org-facing programs: SAM.gov/UEI registration is complete.

## Our recommendation

Take the partnership-and-shepherding path now (Gateway CAA, USDA 504 files, Frontier), file the small direct applications that are genuinely open to you (Walmart Spark Good, Thrivent, SDOP), resolve the sponsor question within six weeks, and file the CDC's 1023-EZ early so the multi-year clocks start.
